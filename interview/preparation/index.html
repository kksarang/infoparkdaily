How to use the InfoparkDaily job board

  1. Open Jobs and choose a park (Infopark, Technopark, Cyberpark) or leave All Parks.
  2. Tap a filter chip: Freshers, Experienced, Walk-in Interview, Non IT, Internships, Remote or Referral.
  3. Type a role or skill in the search box (“tester”, “React”, “accountant”).
  4. Read the status on each card: Live, Closing or Expired. Skip expired posts.
  5. Open View Details to see roles, experience, location, deadline and the apply method.
  6. Tap the heart to save jobs you want to apply for; they stay in Saved jobs.
  7. Use Share to send an opening to a friend on WhatsApp or Instagram.
  8. Before applying, open the official link on the listing and confirm the job is still open.
Tip

Five minutes every morning on the InfoparkDaily feed beats an hour once a week. Early applicants are more likely to be read before a recruiter has enough candidates.

Fresher track

Finding fresher jobs

  • On InfoparkDaily, use the Freshers and Internships filters, and watch Mass Hiring drives.
  • Search portals for “trainee”, “associate”, “graduate engineer trainee”, “junior”, “intern”, “0–1 years”.
  • Large IT services companies run fresher programmes on their own portals (for example TCS NextStep, Infosys Careers, Wipro Careers).
  • Walk-in drives at Infopark and Technopark often hire support, testing and non-voice process freshers.
  • Your college placement cell and alumni are channels too — ask seniors which companies hired them.

Experienced track

Finding experienced roles

  • On InfoparkDaily, use Experienced and Referral filters.
  • Search by title + main tool (“Senior QA Selenium Kochi”) rather than only the title.
  • Keep Naukri and LinkedIn profiles updated — many experienced hires start with a recruiter messaging you.
  • Ask ex-colleagues in target companies for referrals before applying cold.
  • Follow target companies on LinkedIn; many announce openings in posts before portals.

Walk-in interviews

  • Use the Walk-in filter on InfoparkDaily. Each listing shows date, time, venue and roles.
  • Confirm the venue and date on the official notice the same morning — walk-ins get rescheduled.
  • Carry printed resumes, ID proof, photos and certificate copies (originals only if asked).
  • Arrive 30–45 minutes early; registrations often close before the posted end time.

Why one site is never enough

Reality check

Jobs are distributed across many places. Some employers publish only on their own careers site, some only on LinkedIn, some only through a recruitment agency, and some just email a park list or share a WhatsApp post. Using InfoparkDaily as your daily hub and checking a few other sources weekly gives you the widest view of the market.

  1. Daily: InfoparkDaily feed + alerts
  2. 2–3× a week: LinkedIn / Naukri / Indeed alerts
  3. Weekly: target company career pages
  4. Weekly: park job boards
  5. Always: referrals from your network

Other platforms — what each is for

Open a platform to see what jobs it has, how to set it up, how to search and apply, alerts, pros and cons, and practical tips.

LinkedIn Jobs Product companies, MNCs, startups, experienced roles

A professional network with a job board built in. Recruiters use it two ways: they post jobs, and they search member profiles to find people who never applied.

Jobs you usually find
Software, data, product, design, marketing, sales and leadership roles. Strong for 1+ years of experience; fewer walk-in or entry-level support roles.
Create an account
  1. Sign up at linkedin.com with an email you check daily (not a college ID you will lose).
  2. Confirm the email and phone number.
  3. Add your current city — Kochi, Thiruvananthapuram or Kozhikode — so local recruiters find you.
Complete your profile
  • Clear face photo and a headline that says what you do: “Java Developer · Spring Boot · 2 yrs” or “B.Tech CSE 2026 · Python · SQL · Looking for fresher roles”.
  • About section: 3–5 lines on skills, domain and what you want next.
  • Experience and Education with dates that match your resume exactly.
  • Add relevant skills (LinkedIn lets you list many; pin your strongest).
  • Turn on Open to Work. Choose “Recruiters only” if your current employer should not see it.
Search for vacancies
  • Search a role (“QA Engineer”) and a location (“Kochi, Kerala, India”).
  • Filter Date posted → Past week, Experience level (Internship, Entry level, Associate, Mid-Senior) and On-site / Hybrid / Remote.
  • Save searches you repeat.
How to apply
  • Easy Apply sends your LinkedIn profile + uploaded resume inside LinkedIn.
  • Apply opens the company’s own careers site — finish the form there or nothing is sent.
Job alerts

On a search results page, switch on Set alert. You get daily or weekly emails and app notifications.

Advantages
  • Recruiters contact you directly
  • See who works at the company (referrals)
  • Company pages, posts and employee count help research
Limitations
  • Easy Apply posts get hundreds of applicants
  • Some jobs are reposts that are already filled
  • Fake “recruiters” exist — verify before sharing documents
Practical tips
  • Apply in the first 2–3 days a post is live.
  • After applying, send one short note to the recruiter or hiring manager. Do not spam connections.
  • Post about a project once a month — profiles with activity show up more.

Naukri Indian IT services, BFSI, mid-level roles, recruiter search

India’s largest job portal. Besides job posts, recruiters search Naukri’s resume database (Resdex), so a complete, recently updated profile matters as much as applying.

Jobs you usually find
IT services, banking, insurance, sales, accounts, support and mid-level tech roles across India. Many Infopark and Technopark service companies recruit here.
Create an account
  1. Register at naukri.com and choose Fresher or Experienced — this changes the form.
  2. Verify email and mobile.
  3. Upload your resume as PDF or DOCX.
Complete your profile
  • Resume headline (one line): “Manual & automation tester · Selenium, Java · 3 yrs · Kochi”.
  • Key skills: add the exact tools from job posts you target.
  • Fill employment, education, projects, current & expected CTC, notice period and preferred locations.
  • Profile summary in 3–4 lines.
  • Edit something small every week — recruiters often filter by recently active profiles.
Search for vacancies
  • Search skills or titles plus location and years of experience.
  • Use filters: Freshness, Salary, Work mode, Industry, Company type.
  • Read the “Job description” and “Role” sections fully; many posts list more years than they truly need.
How to apply
  • Apply sends your Naukri profile to the employer.
  • Apply on company site takes you away — complete that form.
Job alerts

Click Create job alert on search results, or set preferences in your profile for recommended jobs.

Advantages
  • Huge volume of Indian openings
  • Recruiters find you without you applying
  • Clear CTC and notice-period fields
Limitations
  • Paid add-ons are promoted; none are required to get a job
  • Fake callers pretend to be “Naukri” and ask for fees
  • Many duplicate agency posts
Practical tips
  • Naukri never needs you to pay a recruiter. Anyone asking for a “registration fee” is a scam.
  • Keep the profile CTC and notice period honest — they are checked later.
  • Write the headline the way recruiters search: role + main tool + years + city.

Open Naukri ↗

Indeed Non-IT, support, sales, accounts, walk-ins, small companies

A search engine for jobs. It shows jobs posted directly on Indeed and jobs collected from company sites and other boards.

Jobs you usually find
Customer support, BPO, sales, accounting, admin, hospital and retail roles, plus some IT jobs from smaller companies.
Create an account
  1. Sign in with email or Google.
  2. Upload a resume or create an Indeed resume.
  3. Choose whether employers can find your resume.
Complete your profile
  • Job title you want, desired pay and locations.
  • Work experience, education and skills.
  • Answer the “ready to work” preferences honestly.
Search for vacancies
  • Type What (role) and Where (city).
  • Filter by Date posted, Remote, Salary estimate and Job type.
  • Open the company name to read reviews before applying.
How to apply
  • Easily apply sends your Indeed resume and screener answers.
  • Other posts redirect to the employer’s site.
Job alerts

Below search results, add your email to get new jobs for this search by email.

Advantages
  • Simple, quick applications
  • Good for non-IT and entry roles
  • Company reviews and salary ranges
Limitations
  • Older listings sometimes stay visible
  • Scam posts appear — verify the employer
  • Less recruiter outreach than LinkedIn or Naukri
Practical tips
  • Answer screener questions carefully; they filter you before a human reads anything.
  • Check the post date and the employer’s website before you apply.

Monster India rebranded as foundit. It works like Naukri: post-based applications plus a resume database recruiters search.

Jobs you usually find
Experienced roles in IT, banking, sales, operations and Gulf/overseas openings.
Create an account
  1. Register with email and phone.
  2. Upload your resume and pick your industry and function.
Complete your profile
  • Headline, key skills, experience, CTC, notice period, locations.
  • Keep it consistent with your Naukri and LinkedIn data.
Search for vacancies
  • Search by skill or title, filter by experience, location and salary.
How to apply
  • Apply with your profile, or go to the employer’s site when redirected.
Job alerts

Save a search or set job alerts from your dashboard.

Advantages
  • Less crowded than the biggest portals
  • Useful extra reach for experienced candidates
Limitations
  • Smaller fresher volume
  • Profile data can go stale if you forget it
Practical tips
  • Create it once, then update every few weeks while you are searching.

Internshala Students, internships, first jobs

A platform focused on internships and fresher jobs, with short application questions instead of long forms.

Jobs you usually find
Internships (remote and in-office), fresher roles in development, marketing, content, design, HR and sales.
Create an account
  1. Register as a student/fresher.
  2. Fill education, skills, projects and links (GitHub, portfolio).
Complete your profile
  • Add projects with links.
  • Write a short, specific “Why should you be hired?” answer you can adapt.
Search for vacancies
  • Filter by profile, location, work-from-home, stipend and duration.
How to apply
  • Answer the employer’s questions; some include a small assignment.
Job alerts

Turn on preferences and email notifications for matching internships.

Advantages
  • Built for people with no experience
  • Assignments let you prove skill
Limitations
  • Some internships are unpaid or very low stipend
  • Paid trainings are promoted — optional, not a hiring requirement
Practical tips
  • Never pay an employer to join an internship.
  • A completed internship with a reference beats three certificates.

Google job search Finding the same job across many boards at once

Search “jobs in Kochi” or “react developer jobs Trivandrum” and Google often shows a jobs panel pulled from many sites. Google does not hire — it links to the source.

Jobs you usually find
Whatever the source sites publish: portals, company pages and aggregators.
Create an account
  1. No separate account needed; signing in to Google may save searches.
Complete your profile
  • None — your profile lives on the site you apply through.
Search for vacancies
  • Use role + city, then filters like Date posted and Type.
  • Open “Apply on” options and prefer the company’s own site.
How to apply
  • Click through to the source and apply there.
Job alerts

Availability changes; rely on the alerts of the portal you finally use.

Advantages
  • One search covers many sites
  • Shows which boards carry the same job
Limitations
  • Can surface outdated or copied listings
  • You still apply elsewhere
Practical tips
  • When the same job shows on many sites, apply on the employer’s own page if it exists.

Official company career pages Most accurate source; jobs that never reach portals

Most mid-size and large companies run their own careers page or an ATS portal (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Zoho Recruit, Darwinbox and others).

Jobs you usually find
Every role the company chooses to publish — including ones not posted on portals.
Create an account
  1. Many portals ask you to create a candidate account per company.
  2. Save your password; you will check application status there.
Complete your profile
  • Upload resume; the portal auto-fills fields — always correct what it parsed wrong.
Search for vacancies
  • Search “Company name careers” and confirm the domain matches the company’s main website.
  • Mass-hiring examples: TCS NextStep, Infosys Careers, Wipro Careers.
How to apply
  • Fill every field, answer knock-out questions honestly, submit and save the confirmation email.
Job alerts

Many portals have “Job alerts” or “Talent community” sign-ups.

Advantages
  • Official and current
  • Your application goes straight into the employer’s ATS
Limitations
  • A new account for each company
  • Long forms
Practical tips
  • Bookmark career pages of your 20 target companies and check them weekly.

Government recruitment portals Public-sector, PSC and central government jobs

Government jobs follow notifications, exams and rank lists — a separate track from private IT hiring.

Jobs you usually find
State and central government posts, PSUs, and some government IT/technical positions.
Create an account

Complete your profile
  • Enter details exactly as in certificates — mismatches can disqualify you.
Search for vacancies

Limitations

What ATS means

An Applicant Tracking System is software companies use to collect applications, store resumes, move candidates through stages (Applied → Screening → Interview → Offer) and send emails. Common ones include Workday, SuccessFactors, Greenhouse, Lever, Zoho Recruit and Darwinbox. Smaller companies may simply use a shared inbox and a spreadsheet.

  1. Your resume is uploaded
  2. ATS parses text into fields
  3. Knock-out questions are checked
  4. Recruiter searches / filters
  5. A human reads shortlisted resumes

Resume parsing

Parsing means the ATS reads your file and fills fields: name, email, phone, job titles, companies, dates, education and skills. If parsing fails, your profile may look empty to a recruiter even though your PDF looks perfect.

Keyword and job-description matching

  • Recruiters search the ATS by keywords like “Selenium”, “React”, “Tally”, “IELTS” — your resume must contain the exact words.
  • Use both forms of common terms once: “Search Engine Optimization (SEO)”.
  • Put keywords where they make sense: summary, skills and inside bullets.
  • Don’t paste the JD in white text — recruiters see the parsed text and will reject you.

Reality check

An ATS checker (including ours) is a preparation tool. It estimates how readable your resume is and how many JD keywords it contains. It is not the employer’s actual ATS, it does not log in to any company system, and a high score does not guarantee shortlisting. Every employer configures its ATS differently, and in most companies a human recruiter still makes the decision.

Step by step: how screening works

  1. Data collection. The ATS stores your resume, form answers, source (e.g. “LinkedIn”, “Referral”) and the date you applied.
  2. Resume parsing. The system extracts name, contact, titles, employers, dates, education and skills.
  3. Knock-out / eligibility filters. Hard rules set by HR: minimum degree, year of passing, location, notice period, work authorisation, minimum years. Fail one and you may be auto-rejected or sorted to the bottom.
  4. Keyword search and ranking. Recruiters search terms like “Selenium AND Java AND Kochi” or look at a match score the ATS calculates.
  5. Recruiter screening. A person skims the top resumes — often under a minute each in the first pass — checking role fit, stability, gaps and location.
  6. Experience and skill matching. Recruiter compares your projects or past roles with must-have skills from the hiring manager.
  7. Shortlist review. The hiring manager looks at the shortlist and picks who gets a call or test.
  8. Status update. Candidates move to Screening/Interview, Hold, or Rejected — but many systems never send the rejection email.

Shortlisting & application status

Shortlisted, rejected, on hold or no response — what each outcome means and what to do next.

Open chapterClose chapter

What you do

  • Read status signals correctly
  • Follow up politely once or twice
  • Keep applying — never wait on one company

What the company does

  • Moves candidates into stages
  • Often sends no update for rejections

After screening, your application lands in one of four states. You may never be told which one — so learn the signals.

✓

1. Shortlisted

What it means: your profile matched and someone wants to talk or test you.

Signals: HR call, email with test link, interview invite, portal status “In review / Interview”.

Do next: reply within a few hours, confirm the slot, research the company, prepare (Chapters 9–12).

✕

2. Rejected

What it means: not progressing for this opening.

Possible reasons: missing must-have skills, eligibility rule, stronger candidates, role filled internally, budget.

Do next: thank them if they wrote to you, ask briefly for feedback, improve your resume, apply to other roles there later.

⏸

3. On hold

What it means: you are acceptable but not first choice, or the hiring itself is paused.

Possible reasons: waiting for other candidates, budget approval, client project delays.

Do next: follow up after 2 weeks; keep interviewing elsewhere.

…

4. No response

What it means: the most common outcome. Usually a silent rejection, sometimes simply unread.

Possible reasons: hundreds of applicants, inbox overload, the role closed, wrong channel.

Do next: one follow-up after 7–10 working days; if nothing in 3–4 weeks, move on.

Reality check

Candidates do not always receive a rejection message. Many companies only contact people they shortlist. Silence after 3–4 weeks usually means the application is not moving — it says nothing about your worth.

Practice: IT technical (with answers)

Write a function to check whether a string is a palindrome.

Sample answer

def is_palindrome(s):
    s = ''.join(c.lower() for c in s if c.isalnum())
    return s == s[::-1]

is_palindrome("Malayalam")  # True

Why it works: Handles case and spaces; mention time complexity O(n).

Find the second highest salary from an employees table.

Sample answer

SELECT MAX(salary) AS second_highest
FROM employees
WHERE salary < (SELECT MAX(salary) FROM employees);

Why it works: Works even with duplicate top salaries. Alternative: DENSE_RANK().

Find duplicate emails in a users table.

Sample answer

SELECT email, COUNT(*) AS times
FROM users
GROUP BY email
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;

Why it works: GROUP BY + HAVING is a favourite fresher question.

== converts types before comparing ('5' == 5 is true); === checks value and type ('5' === 5 is false). Prefer ===.

More IT questions (with answers)

Method overloading vs overriding?

Sample answer

Overloading: same method name, different parameters, in the same class (resolved at compile time). Overriding: a subclass redefines a parent method with the same signature (resolved at runtime).

Stack vs queue?

Sample answer

Stack is LIFO (last in, first out) — like undo. Queue is FIFO (first in, first out) — like a ticket line.

WHERE vs HAVING in SQL?

Sample answer

WHERE filters rows before grouping; HAVING filters groups after GROUP BY (e.g. HAVING COUNT(*) > 1).

What do HTTP status codes 200, 201, 400, 401, 403, 404 and 500 mean?

Sample answer

200 OK · 201 Created · 400 Bad Request · 401 Unauthorized (not logged in) · 403 Forbidden (no permission) · 404 Not Found · 500 Internal Server Error.

Which HTTP methods are idempotent?

Sample answer

GET, PUT and DELETE (and HEAD, OPTIONS) — repeating them has the same effect. POST is not idempotent.

Basic Git workflow?

Severity vs priority? (QA)

Severity = impact on the system (crash = high). Priority = how soon to fix (a typo in the company name on the homepage is low severity but high priority).

Smoke vs sanity vs regression testing?

Smoke: quick check that the build’s main features work. Sanity: focused check after a small fix. Regression: re-testing existing features to make sure new changes didn’t break them.

Process vs thread?

A process is a running program with its own memory. Threads run inside a process and share its memory, so they’re lighter but need synchronisation.

Fresher track

Fresher technical questions

What are the four pillars of OOP?

Sample answer

Encapsulation (keep data and methods together, hide internals), Abstraction (show only essentials — e.g. an interface), Inheritance (a class reuses another — Car extends Vehicle), Polymorphism (same method, different behaviour — method overriding).

Difference between an array and a linked list?

Sample answer

Arrays store items in contiguous memory with O(1) index access but costly inserts in the middle. Linked lists use nodes with pointers: O(1) insert once you have the node, but O(n) access by index.

What is a primary key vs a foreign key?

Sample answer

A primary key uniquely identifies each row (e.g. student_id). A foreign key in another table refers to it (e.g. marks.student_id) to link records.

Explain INNER JOIN vs LEFT JOIN.

Sample answer

INNER JOIN returns only rows with matches in both tables. LEFT JOIN returns all rows from the left table, with NULLs where the right table has no match.

What happens when you type a URL in the browser?

Sample answer

DNS lookup → TCP (and TLS for HTTPS) connection → HTTP request → server processes → response with HTML → browser parses and requests CSS/JS/images → renders the page.

Walk me through your final-year project.

Sample answer

“We built an attendance system for our college using Django and MySQL. Problem: paper registers took 10 minutes per class. I built the backend APIs and the reports module. The hardest part was preventing duplicate marking, which we solved with a unique constraint on student + session. It’s used by three departments.”

Why it works: Problem → role → tech → challenge → result.

Experienced track

Experienced technical questions

How would you design a URL shortener?

Sample answer

Requirements first (scale, custom aliases, expiry). API: POST /shorten, GET /{code}. Generate short codes using base62 encoding of an ID. Store code→URL in a key-value store with a cache in front for hot links. Add rate limiting and analytics asynchronously via a queue.

Why it works: Start simple, then scale — interviewers want trade-offs.

An API that was fast is now slow in production. How do you investigate?

Sample answer

Check monitoring for when it started and what changed (deployments, traffic). Look at latency by endpoint, DB slow-query logs and missing indexes, external dependency timings, connection pool limits, memory/GC. Reproduce in staging, fix, then add an alert.

Why it works: Structured: measure → isolate → fix → prevent.

How do you ensure code quality in your team?

Sample answer

Pull-request reviews with a checklist, unit and integration tests in CI, linting, small PRs, and clear definition of done. In my last team, adding PR templates and a test-coverage gate reduced production bugs by about 30% in two quarters.

Explain database indexing and when it can hurt.

Sample answer

An index (typically a B-tree) speeds up lookups and sorting on indexed columns. It costs storage and slows inserts/updates, and unused or low-selectivity indexes add overhead with no benefit.

Tell me about a production incident you handled.

Sample answer

Use STAR: Situation (payments failing for 20 minutes), Task (restore service), Action (identified a bad config deploy, rolled back, added config validation in CI), Result (no repeat in 12 months; wrote the post-mortem).

Why did you choose microservices (or not) in your last project?

Sample answer

Explain the context honestly: team size, deployment independence, scaling needs — and the costs: network calls, distributed tracing, data consistency. Senior candidates show they understand trade-offs.

Live coding: a winning routine

  1. Repeat the problem in your own words and confirm inputs/outputs.
  2. Ask about edge cases: empty input, duplicates, very large numbers.
  3. Explain a brute-force approach first.
  4. Improve it and state time/space complexity.
  5. Write clean code with meaningful names.
  6. Dry-run with a small example and fix bugs out loud.

More questions

Fresher track

What is normalisation?

Sample answer

Organising tables to reduce duplication: 1NF (atomic values), 2NF (no partial dependency on a composite key), 3NF (no transitive dependency). Example: store customer details once in a customers table, not in every order row.

What is an API?

Sample answer

A defined way for programs to talk. A REST API exposes URLs like GET /students/12 that return data, usually JSON.

Shallow vs deep copy in Python?

Sample answer

A shallow copy copies the outer object but shares inner objects; a deep copy (copy.deepcopy) copies everything recursively.

Experienced track

How would you add caching to a slow endpoint?

Sample answer

Measure first. Cache read-heavy, rarely changing data (e.g. Redis) with a sensible TTL; invalidate on writes; watch hit rate; avoid caching per-user sensitive data without keys.

How do you handle a breaking API change?

Sample answer

Version the API (/v2), keep the old version for a deprecation period, communicate timelines, and monitor usage before removal.

How do you estimate a task?

Sample answer

Break it into small parts, estimate each, add time for testing and review, call out unknowns and risks, and re-estimate after a spike if uncertainty is high.

CTC is not your monthly salary

CTC (cost to company) is everything the company spends on you in a year — including employer PF, gratuity, insurance and sometimes bonuses you may not receive monthly. Your in-hand (take-home) salary is lower.

Current CTC & expected CTC

Fresher track

  • Freshers have no current CTC — say “I’m a fresher”.
  • Most fresher salaries are fixed per batch or role band; negotiation room is small.
  • Research the band for the role (posting, AmbitionBox, seniors).
  • Say: “I’m comfortable with the standard fresher package for this role.” If asked a number, give the posted range.
  • Focus on learning, training and conversion (for internships).

Experienced track

  • State current CTC accurately: fixed + variable separately (“₹6.2 LPA fixed, ₹0.5 LPA variable”).
  • Expected CTC: base it on market range, your skills and the role level. Job switches in Indian IT often bring a higher hike than annual appraisals — but it varies by company and skill.
  • Give a number or narrow range: “₹8–8.5 LPA fixed.”
  • If you have another offer, mention it honestly and only if true.
  • Negotiate the whole package: joining bonus, notice buyout, relocation, WFH, title.

Negotiation reply when the offer is below expectation

Tip

Moving your PF (EPF) to the new employer

  1. Your UAN (Universal Account Number) stays the same for your whole career — give it to the new employer, don’t create a new one.
  2. Make sure your UAN is linked with Aadhaar, PAN and bank account on the EPFO member portal.
  3. After the new employer starts contributions, check your service history and passbook on the EPFO member portal.
  4. If the old balance hasn’t moved, use “One Member – One EPF Account (Transfer Request)” on the portal and follow the steps.

Feedback request email

Reality check

Many companies won’t share detailed feedback for legal or time reasons. Ask once, politely, and don’t take silence personally.

Improve: a self-review after every interview

  1. Within an hour, write down every question you remember.
  2. Mark each: answered well / partly / badly.
  3. For every “badly”, write the correct answer and practise it aloud.
  4. Update your resume if a question showed it overstated something.
  5. Do one mock interview a week with a friend or senior.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Is InfoparkDaily a recruitment agency?

No. InfoparkDaily is an independent community platform that collects public job openings and career resources. You apply through the method on each listing — the employer’s email, form or careers page.

Does InfoparkDaily list every job in Kerala?

No platform lists every job. InfoparkDaily is a strong starting point for Kerala IT park and community openings; also check LinkedIn, Naukri, company career pages and park boards.

How long does the hiring process usually take?

Anywhere from one day (walk-in drives) to several weeks for most private roles. Mass fresher hiring and senior roles can take longer, and government recruitment can take months.

Why didn’t I get any reply after applying?

Many employers only contact shortlisted candidates. Follow up once after 7–10 working days; if there’s no response in 3–4 weeks, focus on other applications.

Does a high ATS checker score guarantee an interview?

No. An ATS checker estimates readability and keyword match. It isn’t the employer’s system, and recruiters still make the decision.

Should a fresher negotiate salary?

Fresher salaries are often fixed per batch or role band, so there’s little room. Focus on learning, the role and training; negotiate only if you have a clear reason, such as a competing offer.

What is the difference between CTC and in-hand salary?

CTC includes everything the company spends on you — including employer PF, gratuity and benefits. In-hand is what reaches your bank after deductions like PF, professional tax and income tax.

Should I resign before getting an offer letter?

No. Resign only after you have a written offer that you’ve accepted, and ideally after background verification has started without issues.

Is it okay to pay for a job or interview?

No. Genuine employers and recruiters don’t charge candidates. Any request for a registration, training or document fee is a strong scam signal.

How often should I check for new jobs?

Daily if possible. A quick check of the InfoparkDaily feed and alerts each morning, plus portal alerts and target company pages weekly, keeps you early.
