HR round HR & behavioural

HR round

Asked in almost every interview — phone screens, walk-ins and final HR rounds.

20 questions
1Tell me about yourself.Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Use present → past → proof → future in 60–90 seconds: “I’m a 2026 B.Tech CSE graduate focused on testing. In my final-year project I wrote 120 test cases and automated login checks with Selenium. I want to start my career as a QA engineer in a product team like yours.”

2Why do you want to join our company?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Connect their work to your skills and goals: “You build logistics software for UAE and UK clients. I tested a delivery-tracking app in my project and your team uses Selenium and Jira, which I already know.”

3Why should we hire you?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Match 2–3 must-haves from the JD with proof, then add attitude: “I already know manual testing, Jira and SQL, I’ve automated basic flows, and I learn quickly — I picked up Postman in a week for my project.”

4What are your strengths?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Pick one or two relevant strengths and prove each with a short example. Avoid a list of adjectives.

5What is your greatest weakness?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Give a real but non-critical weakness and what you are doing about it: “I used to take on too much without asking for help. Now I timebox problems and ask after 30 minutes.”

6Where do you see yourself in five years?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Show ambition aligned with the company: “Growing into a senior engineer who owns features end to end and mentors juniors.” Avoid saying you plan to leave.

7Why did you choose this field?Fresher

Sample answer

Give a genuine reason with a moment that sparked interest — a project, a course, an internship — and what you enjoy about the work.

8Your CGPA is low. Why?Fresher

Sample answer

Be honest, show improvement and redirect to strengths: “My first-year grades were low while I adjusted; I improved to 7.6 in later semesters and focused on hands-on projects.”

9Are you willing to relocate or work in shifts?Fresher

Sample answer

Answer clearly and honestly. If yes, say yes. If partly, say what you can do: “I can relocate to Kochi; I prefer day shift but can manage rotational shifts.”

10Are you planning higher studies?Fresher

Sample answer

If not soon, say so: “Not in the near future. I want industry experience first; any learning will be part-time and related to my work.”

11Why are you leaving your current job?Experienced

Sample answer

Stay positive and forward-looking: growth, scale, learning, domain. Never criticise your employer or manager.

12What is your current CTC and expected CTC?Experienced

Sample answer

State current CTC accurately (fixed and variable separately) and give a researched expected range: “₹6.2 LPA fixed; I’m expecting ₹8–8.5 LPA based on the role and market.”

13What is your notice period? Can you join earlier?Experienced

Sample answer

Give the exact number of days and whether early release or buyout is possible. Don’t promise what you can’t deliver.

14Do you have any other offers?Experienced

Sample answer

Be truthful. If yes: “I have one offer, but this role is my preference because of the product work.” Never invent an offer.

15Why have you changed jobs frequently?Experienced

Sample answer

Give brief, factual reasons (project closure, relocation, growth) and show you are looking for stability now.

16Explain the gap in your resume.Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Brief reason plus what you did productively: courses, certifications, projects, family responsibilities. Keep it honest and short.

17How do you handle pressure or tight deadlines?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Explain your method — prioritise, break work into steps, communicate early — and give one real example with a result.

18What do you know about our company?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Mention what they do, key products or clients, size, location and something recent (news, launch). Shows you researched.

19Do you have any questions for us?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Always ask 2–3: “What does success look like in the first 90 days?”, “How is the team structured?”, “What are the next steps?”

20Are you comfortable with a service agreement (bond)?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Ask for the details before committing: duration, amount and what it covers. Agree only after reading the terms in writing.

10 questions

Show you used facts and found a middle path, without blaming. End with the positive outcome.

4Describe a time you met a very tight deadline.Fresher & experienced

5Tell me about something you learned quickly.Fresher & experienced

Talk privately to understand causes, set clear expectations, offer support, check in regularly, and escalate only after support fails.

7Tell me about a decision you made with incomplete information.Experienced

Explain the options, why you chose one (often the reversible or lowest-risk option), and the outcome.

8How do you prioritise when everything is urgent?Experienced

Sample answer

Rank by impact and deadline, confirm the top priority with your manager, communicate what will slip, and protect focus time.

9How do you mentor juniors?Experienced

Pair programming, code reviews with explanations, small ownership tasks, regular 1:1s — give an example of someone who improved.

10Tell me about a project you owned end to end.Experienced

Technical round Core CS

OOP & CS fundamentals

Asked of almost every software fresher, whatever the language.

9 questions
1What are the four pillars of OOP?Fresher

Sample answer

Encapsulation (bundle data and methods, hide internals), Abstraction (expose only essentials), Inheritance (reuse a parent class), Polymorphism (one interface, many behaviours — e.g. method overriding).

2Method overloading vs overriding?Fresher

Sample answer

Overloading: same name, different parameters, same class — resolved at compile time. Overriding: subclass redefines a parent method with the same signature — resolved at runtime.

3Abstract class vs interface?Fresher

Sample answer

An abstract class can have state, constructors and implemented methods; a class extends only one. An interface defines a contract; a class can implement many. (Modern Java interfaces can also have default methods.)

4Process vs thread?Fresher

Sample answer

A process is an independent running program with its own memory. Threads run inside a process and share its memory — lighter, but need synchronisation.

5What is a deadlock?Fresher

Sample answer

Two or more threads wait forever for resources held by each other. Conditions: mutual exclusion, hold and wait, no preemption, circular wait. Prevent by ordering lock acquisition.

6What happens when you type a URL in a browser?Fresher

Sample answer

DNS lookup → TCP connection (+ TLS for HTTPS) → HTTP request → server response → browser parses HTML, loads CSS/JS/images → renders the page.

7Compiler vs interpreter?Fresher

Sample answer

A compiler translates the whole program to machine/byte code before running; an interpreter executes code line by line. Java compiles to bytecode which the JVM runs (with JIT compilation).

8What is the difference between stack and heap memory?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Stack holds method calls and local variables (fast, automatic cleanup, limited size). Heap holds objects created at runtime (larger, managed by garbage collection in Java/Python).

9Explain SOLID principles.Experienced

Sample answer

Single responsibility, Open/closed (extend without modifying), Liskov substitution (subtypes usable as base types), Interface segregation (small specific interfaces), Dependency inversion (depend on abstractions).

Technical round Core CS

Data structures & algorithms

Coding rounds and whiteboard questions. Always state time and space complexity.

10 questions
1Array vs linked list?Fresher

Sample answer

Array: contiguous memory, O(1) index access, costly insertion in the middle. Linked list: nodes with pointers, O(1) insert/delete at a known node, O(n) access by index.

2Stack vs queue?Fresher

Sample answer

Stack is LIFO (push/pop — undo, recursion). Queue is FIFO (enqueue/dequeue — task scheduling, BFS).

3How do you reverse a string?Fresher

Sample answer

Python: s[::-1]. Manually: two pointers swapping from both ends until they meet — O(n) time.

4Find the second largest number in an array.Fresher

Sample answer

Track first and second in one pass: if x > first, second = first and first = x; else if x > second and x ≠ first, second = x. O(n) time, O(1) space.

5What is binary search and its complexity?Fresher

Sample answer

On a sorted array, compare with the middle element and discard half each step. O(log n) time.

6What is a hash map and its average lookup time?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

A key-value structure using a hash function to find buckets. Average O(1) insert and lookup; worst case O(n) with many collisions.

7Check if two strings are anagrams.Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Count characters of both (dictionary or 26-length array) and compare counts — O(n). Sorting both and comparing is O(n log n).

8Find two numbers in an array that add up to a target.Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Use a hash map: for each x, check if target − x was seen; if yes, return the pair; else store x. O(n) time.

9Detect a cycle in a linked list.Experienced

Sample answer

Floyd’s algorithm: slow pointer moves 1, fast moves 2. If they meet, there’s a cycle. O(n) time, O(1) space.

10BFS vs DFS — when would you use each?Experienced

Sample answer

BFS (queue) explores level by level — shortest path in unweighted graphs. DFS (stack/recursion) goes deep — cycle detection, topological sort, exploring all paths.

Technical round Core CS

Linux & networking

For support, DevOps, network and system admin roles.

8 questions
1Common Linux commands you use?Fresher

Sample answer

ls, cd, pwd, cp, mv, rm, cat, less, grep, find, chmod, chown, ps, top, df -h, du -sh, tail -f.

2What does chmod 755 file mean?Fresher

Sample answer

Owner can read/write/execute (7); group and others can read/execute (5).

3TCP vs UDP?Fresher

Sample answer

TCP is connection-oriented and reliable (web, email). UDP is connectionless and faster with no delivery guarantee (video calls, DNS queries).

4What is DNS?Fresher

Sample answer

The system that translates domain names (example.com) into IP addresses.

5What is an IP address and subnet mask?Fresher

Sample answer

An IP identifies a device on a network; the subnet mask (e.g. 255.255.255.0 or /24) defines which part is the network and which is the host.

6Name the OSI model layers.Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Physical, Data Link, Network, Transport, Session, Presentation, Application.

7Common ports: 22, 80, 443, 3306?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

22 SSH, 80 HTTP, 443 HTTPS, 3306 MySQL.

8A server is slow. How do you troubleshoot?Experienced

Sample answer

Check CPU and memory (top), disk space and I/O (df -h, iostat), network, recent logs and deployments; find the bottleneck before changing anything.

Technical round Core CS

Cybersecurity basics

Security fundamentals for developers and security analysts.

6 questions
1What is the CIA triad?Fresher

Sample answer

Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability — the three core goals of information security.

2Encryption vs hashing?Fresher

Sample answer

Encryption is reversible with a key (protect data in transit/at rest). Hashing is one-way (store passwords with a salted, slow hash like bcrypt or Argon2).

3What is phishing?Fresher

Sample answer

Tricking people with fake emails, messages or sites into revealing credentials or installing malware.

4What are SQL injection and XSS?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

SQL injection inserts malicious SQL through input — prevent with parameterised queries. XSS injects scripts into pages — prevent with output encoding and Content Security Policy.

5What is multi-factor authentication?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Requiring two or more factors — something you know, have or are — to log in.

6How would you respond to a suspected breach?Experienced

Sample answer

Contain (isolate systems), preserve evidence, assess scope, eradicate the cause, recover, notify as required, and run a post-incident review.

Technical round Core CS

Git & version control

Asked in most developer and tester interviews.

6 questions
1git fetch vs git pull?Fresher

Sample answer

fetch downloads changes without merging; pull = fetch + merge (or rebase) into your current branch.

2What is a branch?Fresher

Sample answer

An independent line of development; work on features without affecting main, then merge.

3Basic workflow to submit a change?Fresher

Sample answer

git checkout -b feature/x → edit → git add → git commit -m → git push → open a pull request → review → merge.

4Merge vs rebase?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Merge combines histories with a merge commit. Rebase replays your commits on top of another branch for a linear history — don’t rebase shared public branches.

5How do you resolve a merge conflict?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Open the conflicted files, choose or combine the changes between the markers, test, then git add and commit.

6git reset vs git revert?Experienced

Sample answer

reset moves the branch pointer (rewrites history — local use). revert creates a new commit that undoes a previous one (safe for shared branches).

Technical round Programming

Java

Core Java, collections and Spring Boot.

12 questions
1JDK vs JRE vs JVM?Fresher

Sample answer

JVM runs bytecode. JRE = JVM + libraries needed to run Java programs. JDK = JRE + development tools like the compiler (javac).

2Why is String immutable in Java?Fresher

Sample answer

For security, thread safety and the string pool: the same literal can be shared safely. Use StringBuilder for frequent modifications.

3== vs equals()?Fresher

Sample answer

== compares references (same object); equals() compares content when overridden, as in String.

4ArrayList vs LinkedList?Fresher

Sample answer

ArrayList: dynamic array, fast random access, slower inserts in the middle. LinkedList: doubly linked list, fast insert/delete at ends, slow index access.

5Checked vs unchecked exceptions?Fresher

Sample answer

Checked exceptions (IOException) must be handled or declared at compile time. Unchecked (RuntimeException, NullPointerException) are not enforced by the compiler.

6HashMap vs ConcurrentHashMap vs Hashtable?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

HashMap is not thread-safe and allows one null key. Hashtable is synchronised (legacy, slow). ConcurrentHashMap is thread-safe with fine-grained locking and no null keys/values.

7What does the final keyword do?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

final variable can’t be reassigned; final method can’t be overridden; final class can’t be extended.

8What is the Stream API? Give an example.Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Functional processing of collections: list.stream().filter(n -> n % 2 == 0).map(n -> n * n).toList();

9What is dependency injection in Spring?Experienced

Sample answer

The container creates and supplies objects (beans) a class needs instead of the class creating them — usually through constructor injection — making code loosely coupled and testable.

10@Component vs @Service vs @Repository vs @Controller?Experienced

Sample answer

All register Spring beans. @Service marks business logic, @Repository data access (with exception translation), @Controller/@RestController web endpoints; @Component is generic.

11How does @Transactional work?Experienced

Sample answer

Spring wraps the method in a proxy that starts a transaction and commits on success or rolls back on runtime exceptions. Self-invocation inside the same class bypasses the proxy.

12How do you find a memory leak in a Java service?Experienced

Sample answer

Monitor heap growth, take heap dumps (jmap / JVisualVM / Eclipse MAT), look for objects retained by static collections, caches or unclosed resources, then fix and verify.

Technical round Programming

Python

Core Python plus Django/Flask/FastAPI basics.

11 questions
1List vs tuple vs set vs dictionary?Fresher

Sample answer

List: ordered, mutable. Tuple: ordered, immutable. Set: unordered, unique items. Dict: key-value pairs (insertion-ordered since Python 3.7).

2What are *args and **kwargs?Fresher

Sample answer

*args collects extra positional arguments into a tuple; **kwargs collects extra keyword arguments into a dict.

3What is a list comprehension?Fresher

Sample answer

A concise way to build lists: [x * x for x in range(10) if x % 2 == 0].

4Shallow copy vs deep copy?Fresher

Sample answer

copy.copy copies the outer object but shares nested objects; copy.deepcopy copies everything recursively.

5What does if __name__ == "__main__": mean?Fresher

Sample answer

The block runs only when the file is executed directly, not when imported as a module.

6What is a decorator?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

A function that wraps another function to add behaviour (logging, timing, auth) without changing its code — applied with @decorator.

7What is a generator?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

A function using yield that produces values lazily, one at a time — memory-efficient for large data.

8Why is a mutable default argument dangerous?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

def f(x=[]) shares the same list across calls. Use x=None and create a new list inside.

9What is the GIL?Experienced

Sample answer

In CPython, the Global Interpreter Lock lets only one thread execute Python bytecode at a time. Use multiprocessing or async for CPU-bound parallelism; threads still help with I/O.

10Django: what is the N+1 query problem and how do you fix it?Experienced

Sample answer

Looping over objects and querying related data each time. Fix with select_related (foreign keys) or prefetch_related (many-to-many/reverse relations).

11Django vs Flask vs FastAPI?Experienced

Sample answer

Django: batteries-included (ORM, admin, auth). Flask: minimal and flexible. FastAPI: modern, async, type-hint based with automatic OpenAPI docs.

Technical round Programming

C# & .NET

C#, ASP.NET Core and Entity Framework.

7 questions
1Value type vs reference type in C#?Fresher

Sample answer

Value types (int, struct) hold data directly; reference types (class, string, arrays) hold a reference to data on the heap.

2What is the difference between const and readonly?Fresher

Sample answer

const is a compile-time constant; readonly is set at declaration or in the constructor and can differ per instance.

3What is LINQ?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Language Integrated Query — query collections and databases with C# syntax: users.Where(u => u.Age > 18).Select(u => u.Name).

4What is async/await in C#?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Lets I/O-bound code run without blocking threads; await suspends the method until the Task completes.

5What is middleware in ASP.NET Core?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Components in the request pipeline (routing, authentication, logging) configured in Program.cs, each able to process the request and pass it on.

6Explain dependency injection lifetimes in ASP.NET Core.Experienced

Sample answer

Transient: new instance every time. Scoped: one per request. Singleton: one for the app’s lifetime.

7Entity Framework: tracking vs no-tracking queries?Experienced

Sample answer

Tracking queries keep entities in the change tracker for updates; AsNoTracking() is faster for read-only queries.

Technical round Web & mobile

JavaScript

Core JavaScript for frontend and Node.js roles.

10 questions
1var vs let vs const?Fresher

Sample answer

var is function-scoped and hoisted (initialised as undefined). let and const are block-scoped; const can’t be reassigned (but objects it points to can change).

2== vs ===?Fresher

Sample answer

== converts types before comparing ('5' == 5 is true). === compares value and type ('5' === 5 is false).

3What are truthy and falsy values?Fresher

Sample answer

Falsy: false, 0, -0, 0n, "", null, undefined, NaN. Everything else is truthy, including [] and {}.

4What does typeof null return?Fresher

Sample answer

"object" — a long-standing quirk of JavaScript.

5What is a closure?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

A function that remembers variables from its outer scope even after that scope has finished — used for private state and factories.

6Explain the event loop.Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

JavaScript runs on one thread. Async callbacks wait in queues; when the call stack is empty, the event loop runs microtasks (promises) first, then macrotasks (setTimeout, I/O).

7Promise vs async/await?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Both handle async results. async/await is syntax over promises that reads like synchronous code; use try/catch for errors.

8What is event delegation?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Attaching one listener to a parent and using event.target to handle clicks on many children — efficient and works for elements added later.

9Debounce vs throttle?Experienced

Sample answer

Debounce runs a function after activity stops for a delay (search box). Throttle runs it at most once per interval (scroll handlers).

10How does this work?Experienced

Sample answer

Determined by how a function is called: method call → the object; plain call → undefined in strict mode; arrow functions use this from the surrounding scope; bind/call/apply set it explicitly.

Technical round Web & mobile

React

Components, hooks, state and performance.

8 questions
1Props vs state?Fresher

Sample answer

Props are inputs passed from a parent (read-only in the child). State is data a component owns and updates, causing a re-render.

2What is JSX?Fresher

Sample answer

A syntax extension that looks like HTML inside JavaScript; it compiles to React element calls.

3Why do lists need a key?Fresher

Sample answer

Keys help React identify which items changed, were added or removed. Use stable unique IDs, not array indexes, when the list can change.

4What does useEffect do?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Runs side effects after render (fetching data, subscriptions). The dependency array controls when it re-runs; return a cleanup function to unsubscribe.

5Controlled vs uncontrolled components?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Controlled inputs keep their value in React state via value/onChange. Uncontrolled inputs keep their own DOM state, read with refs.

6What is the virtual DOM?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

A lightweight in-memory representation of the UI. React compares (diffs) new and old trees and updates only the changed parts of the real DOM.

7How do you prevent unnecessary re-renders?Experienced

Sample answer

Keep state close to where it’s used, memoise components with React.memo, stabilise callbacks/values with useCallback/useMemo when profiling shows a need, and avoid creating new objects in props.

8Context vs Redux (or other stores)?Experienced

Sample answer

Context passes values without prop drilling — good for theme or auth. A store (Redux Toolkit, Zustand) suits complex, frequently updated shared state with devtools and middleware.

Technical round Web & mobile

Node.js & backend APIs

Node.js, Express and REST API design.

7 questions
1What is Node.js?Fresher

Sample answer

A JavaScript runtime built on Chrome’s V8 engine for running JavaScript on servers, with non-blocking, event-driven I/O.

2What is middleware in Express?Fresher

Sample answer

Functions that run between the request and the response — e.g. parsing JSON, logging, authentication — calling next() to continue.

3What are the common HTTP methods?Fresher

Sample answer

GET (read), POST (create), PUT (replace), PATCH (partial update), DELETE (remove).

4What do status codes 200, 201, 400, 401, 403, 404 and 500 mean?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

OK, Created, Bad Request, Unauthorized (not authenticated), Forbidden (no permission), Not Found, Internal Server Error.

5What is JWT?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

JSON Web Token — a signed token containing claims, used for stateless authentication. Keep it short-lived and never put secrets in its payload.

6How do you handle errors centrally in Express?Experienced

Sample answer

Use an error-handling middleware with four arguments (err, req, res, next), map known errors to status codes, log details, and return safe messages.

7How would you secure a REST API?Experienced

Sample answer

HTTPS, authentication (JWT/OAuth), authorisation checks per resource, input validation, rate limiting, parameterised queries, secure headers and secrets in environment variables.

Technical round Web & mobile

PHP & Laravel

PHP basics and Laravel framework.

7 questions
1echo vs print in PHP?Fresher

Sample answer

Both output text. echo can take multiple arguments and has no return value; print takes one argument and returns 1.

2GET vs POST?Fresher

Sample answer

GET sends data in the URL (visible, cacheable, for reading). POST sends data in the request body (for submitting forms and changing data).

3Session vs cookie?Fresher

Sample answer

Cookies are stored in the browser; sessions store data on the server and use a cookie with a session ID.

4How do you prevent SQL injection in PHP?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Use prepared statements with PDO or MySQLi and bound parameters — never concatenate user input into SQL.

5What is Eloquent in Laravel?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Laravel’s ORM, where each model maps to a table and relationships are defined as methods (hasMany, belongsTo).

6What are Laravel migrations and seeders?Experienced

Sample answer

Migrations version-control the database schema; seeders insert sample or default data.

7What are queues in Laravel used for?Experienced

Sample answer

Moving slow tasks (emails, reports, image processing) to background workers so requests respond quickly.

Technical round Web & mobile

Android (Kotlin)

Android app development with Kotlin.

7 questions
1Explain the Activity lifecycle.Fresher

Sample answer

onCreate → onStart → onResume (running) → onPause → onStop → onDestroy; onRestart when returning from stopped.

2val vs var in Kotlin?Fresher

Sample answer

val is read-only (cannot be reassigned); var is mutable.

3How does Kotlin handle null safety?Fresher

Sample answer

Types are non-null by default; nullable types use ?. Use ?., the Elvis operator ?:, and avoid !!.

4What is a ViewModel?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

An architecture component that holds UI data and survives configuration changes like rotation.

5What are coroutines?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Kotlin’s lightweight way to write asynchronous code sequentially, using suspend functions and scopes like viewModelScope.

6Explain MVVM in Android.Experienced

Sample answer

View (Activity/Compose UI) observes state from the ViewModel; the ViewModel gets data from a Repository, which talks to network and database.

7How do you reduce app crashes and ANRs?Experienced

Sample answer

Keep heavy work off the main thread, handle nulls and errors, monitor with Crashlytics, add tests, and roll out releases gradually.

Technical round Web & mobile

Flutter

Cross-platform apps with Flutter and Dart.

7 questions
1StatelessWidget vs StatefulWidget?Fresher

Sample answer

Stateless widgets don’t change after building. Stateful widgets hold mutable state and rebuild when setState is called.

2What is hot reload?Fresher

Sample answer

Injects code changes into the running app and rebuilds widgets while keeping app state — fast UI iteration.

3What does the build() method do?Fresher

7How do you maintain brand consistency across a team?Experienced

Use a brand guide with logo rules, colours, typography and templates, keep shared libraries in Figma or Creative Cloud, and review outputs before publishing.

Role-based Core engineering

Mechanical engineering

For design, production, maintenance and quality engineer roles.

8 questions
1Define stress and strain.Fresher

Sample answer

Stress is internal force per unit area (N/mm²). Strain is the change in length divided by the original length (no unit).

2State Hooke’s law.Fresher

Sample answer

Within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain. The constant of proportionality is Young’s modulus.

3What is the factor of safety?Fresher

Sample answer

The ratio of the material’s failure (ultimate or yield) stress to the allowable working stress — a margin for uncertainty.

4Two-stroke vs four-stroke engine?Fresher

Sample answer

A two-stroke engine completes a power cycle in one crankshaft revolution; a four-stroke needs two revolutions. Four-strokes are generally more fuel-efficient and cleaner; two-strokes are simpler and lighter.

5Welding vs brazing vs soldering?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Welding melts the base metals to join them. Brazing uses a filler that melts above about 450 °C without melting the base metal. Soldering uses a filler that melts below about 450 °C.

6Which CAD tools have you used?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Name what you can demonstrate — AutoCAD for 2D drafting, SolidWorks, CATIA or Creo for 3D modelling — and describe a part you designed.

7Preventive vs predictive maintenance?Experienced

Sample answer

Preventive maintenance is time-based (servicing every N hours). Predictive maintenance uses condition data — vibration, temperature, oil analysis — to service only when needed.

8How do you find the root cause of a recurring defect?Experienced

Sample answer

Collect data, use tools like 5 Whys and a fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram, verify the cause with trials, fix it, and add controls so it doesn’t recur.

Civil engineering

7 questions
1What does M20 grade concrete mean?Fresher

Sample answer

Concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 20 N/mm² at 28 days. Its nominal mix is commonly 1 : 1.5 : 3 (cement : sand : aggregate).

2What does a slump test measure?Fresher

Sample answer

The workability (consistency) of fresh concrete.

3Why is curing important?Fresher

Sample answer

It keeps concrete moist so cement hydrates properly, improving strength and durability and reducing cracks. For ordinary Portland cement, at least 7 days of curing is the common minimum.

4One-way vs two-way slab?Fresher

Sample answer

If the longer span divided by the shorter span is more than 2, the slab bends mainly in one direction (one-way). Otherwise it spans both ways (two-way).

5Which software have you used?Fresher & experienced

AutoCAD for drawings, STAAD.Pro or ETABS for structural analysis, Revit for BIM, and MS Project or Primavera for planning — mention projects you used them on.

6How do you ensure quality on site?Fresher & experienced

Inspection checklists, material testing (cube tests, sieve analysis), checking reinforcement before pouring, following approved drawings and keeping records.

7How do you control project cost on site?Experienced

Track quantities against the BOQ, control material wastage, monitor labour productivity, raise variations promptly and review budget versus actuals every week.

Role-based Core engineering

Electrical & electronics

For electrical, maintenance, electronics and instrumentation roles.

8 questions
1State Ohm’s law.Fresher

Sample answer

Voltage equals current multiplied by resistance (V = I × R), at constant temperature.

2AC vs DC?Fresher

Sample answer

AC changes direction periodically; DC flows in one direction. India’s mains supply is about 230 V at 50 Hz.

3What is power factor?Fresher

Sample answer

The ratio of real power (kW) to apparent power (kVA). A low power factor wastes capacity; it is commonly improved with capacitor banks.

4MCB vs RCCB?Fresher

Sample answer

An MCB protects against overload and short circuit. An RCCB detects earth leakage current and protects people from electric shock.

5Star vs delta connection?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

In star, line voltage = √3 × phase voltage and line current = phase current. In delta, line voltage = phase voltage and line current = √3 × phase current.

6Why doesn’t a transformer work on DC?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

It works by mutual induction, which needs a changing magnetic flux. DC produces a constant flux, so no voltage is induced — and the winding can overheat.

7What safety steps do you follow before maintenance?Experienced

Sample answer

Isolate the supply, apply lockout/tagout, test for absence of voltage, use proper PPE, and follow the permit-to-work system.

8What is a PLC and where have you used one?Experienced

Sample answer

A Programmable Logic Controller automates machines using inputs, logic and outputs (often ladder logic). Explain a sequence you programmed or troubleshot.

Role-based Hospitality, retail & logistics

Logistics & supply chain

For warehouse, procurement, logistics coordinator and supply chain roles.

7 questions
1FIFO vs FEFO?Fresher

Sample answer

FIFO (first in, first out) dispatches the oldest stock first. FEFO (first expiry, first out) dispatches the stock that expires soonest — essential for food and medicines.

2What is lead time?Fresher

Sample answer

The time between placing an order and receiving the goods.

3What is an e-way bill?Fresher

Sample answer

An electronic document under GST required for moving goods worth more than ₹50,000 (some states set different thresholds for movement within the state).

4What is inventory turnover?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Cost of goods sold ÷ average inventory. A higher turnover means stock moves faster.

5FOB vs CIF?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

Under FOB the buyer arranges the main freight and insurance, and risk passes once goods are loaded on the vessel. Under CIF the seller pays cost, insurance and freight to the destination port, but risk still passes at loading.

6What is a WMS?Fresher & experienced

Sample answer

A Warehouse Management System that tracks stock locations, receiving, put-away, picking, packing and dispatch.

7How would you reduce logistics costs?Experienced

Sample answer

Consolidate shipments, optimise routes, negotiate carrier rates, improve load utilisation, reduce damages and returns, and track cost per shipment.
