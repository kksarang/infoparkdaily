🎓 Fresher / student plan

No work experience yet? That’s fine — projects, internships and college work all count. Do these steps in order. Most take less than an hour; doing a little each day, you can finish them in 2–4 weeks.

  • 8steps
  • 2–4weeks
  1. Step 1

    Make your first resume

    Pick a simple one-page template. Put your projects, internships and skills near the top — you don’t need work experience.

    30 min Build my resume →
  2. Step 2

    Check it against a job

    Many companies use software (an ATS) to sort resumes first. Paste a job post and see which skills it asks for that your resume doesn’t mention — add them only if you really have them.

    10 min Check my resume →
  3. Step 3

    Find fresher jobs & walk-ins

    Look for jobs with Trainee, Associate, Junior or Intern in the title. Walk-in drives let you go straight to the interview with your resume — good practice even if you’re not selected.

    15 min a day See fresher jobs →
  4. Step 4

    Send a proper application email

    Write a clear subject line and 5–6 short lines, and attach your resume as a PDF. Copy our template and change the details to yours.

    10 min Get email template →
  5. Step 5

    Practise HR & aptitude questions

    Start with “Tell me about yourself”. Then practise aptitude (maths and logic questions) and the basics of your subject.

    30 min a day Practise fresher questions →
  6. Step 6

    Learn & try live code

    Missing a skill that jobs ask for? Learn it free and practise code right in your browser — nothing to install.

    Ongoing Start learning →
  7. Step 7

    Prepare for each interview round

    Most companies have 3–4 rounds: an HR call, a test, a technical interview and a final HR or manager round. Learn what each one checks and how to answer.

    2–3 hrs Open the guide →
  8. Step 8

    Got an offer? Check it first

    Before you say yes, check the in-hand salary (what reaches your bank each month — not the yearly CTC), any bond or penalty, the joining date and the documents they need.

    20 min Read offer tips →

💼 Experienced professional plan

Planning a job change? Do these steps while you’re still working. Start 2–3 months before you want to move — most companies expect a notice period of 30 to 90 days.

  • 7steps
  • 8–12weeks
  1. Step 1

    Update your resume with results

    Show results with numbers (for example, “cut report time by 40%”), not just duties. Keep job titles and dates exactly as in your offer and relieving letters.

    45 min Update my resume →
  2. Step 2

    Match it to the roles you want

    Compare your resume with 2–3 job posts you want and add the skills they ask for that you really have.

    10 min per job Check my resume →
  3. Step 3

    Find experienced roles & referrals

    On InfoparkDaily Jobs, use the Experienced and Referral filters. Also check LinkedIn, Naukri and company career pages.

    15 min a day See experienced jobs →
  4. Step 4

    Reach recruiters and referrers

    Ask former colleagues for referrals and reply to recruiters with short, clear messages. Copy our ready-made emails.

    15 min Get email templates →
  5. Step 5

    Practise technical & managerial questions

    Practise explaining your projects, technical questions for your role, and STAR answers (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for manager rounds.

    30 min a day Practise experienced questions →
  6. Step 6

    Prepare salary & notice-period answers

    Know your current CTC (total yearly pay), the range you expect, your notice period, and whether you can buy it out (pay to leave early).

    30 min Salary & negotiation guide →
  7. Step 7

    Compare offers, then resign right

    Read the offer letter fully and accept in writing — only then resign. Hand over your work properly and collect your relieving letter.

    30 min Offer & resignation guide →

Help

Not sure where to start?

I’m a fresher with no experience. Is this for me?

Yes. Choose Fresher / student above. Every step is written for people applying for their first job — projects and internships count.

I don’t know which job to apply for.

Start with fresher jobs or walk-ins, read a few job descriptions, and pick roles that match your degree and projects. The Learning page shows what each role needs.

I have an interview tomorrow. What should I do?

Practise “Tell me about yourself” and the HR questions, revise your resume line by line, and read the HR call and technical interview chapters.

What is an ATS, and why does it matter?

An ATS (applicant tracking system) is software many companies use to collect and sort resumes before a person reads them. It reads the text of your resume, so use a simple layout and the same skill names the job post uses. Check your resume free.

What do CTC and in-hand salary mean?

CTC (cost to company) is everything the company spends on you in a year, including things like PF and insurance. In-hand is what actually reaches your bank each month after deductions, so it is always less than CTC divided by 12. Ask for the monthly in-hand amount before you accept. Read the offer letter guide.

How do I spot a fake job offer?

Real companies never ask you to pay for an application, interview, training or joining kit. Be careful with offers that come without any interview, only through WhatsApp or a personal email, or that push you to decide quickly. How to check a job is real.

Is everything free? Do I need to sign up?

Everything here is free and works without signing up. Your progress is saved in your browser. Sign in only if you want to save resumes in the Resume Builder.

Is InfoparkDaily the company hiring me?

No. InfoparkDaily lists openings from companies and public sources. Always apply through the method on the job listing, and never pay anyone for a job.
