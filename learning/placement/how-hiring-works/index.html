How fresher hiring works

Campus vs off-campus hiring, the usual rounds, and how service and product companies differ.

← Back toPlacement Prep home Next lesson →A one-page fresher resume that passes ATS

Most freshers in India get their first IT job through one of two routes:

  • Campus hiring — the company visits (or runs an online drive for) your college through the placement cell. Eligibility rules such as minimum marks or no active backlogs are set by the company.
  • Off-campus hiring — you apply directly: company careers pages, national hiring tests, job portals, referrals, walk-in drives and park job boards like Infopark, Technopark and Cyberpark.

The usual rounds

  1. Application screen — your resume and eligibility are checked, often by software (an ATS).
  2. Online assessment — aptitude, logical reasoning, verbal English and, for tech roles, one to three coding questions.
  3. Technical interview — programming basics, your projects, and core subjects such as OOP, DBMS, OS and networks.
  4. HR / managerial interview — communication, attitude, relocation, shifts and joining date.

Not every company runs every round, and some merge technical and HR into one conversation.

Service vs product companies

IT services companies hire freshers in large batches, train them, and place them on client projects. Selection leans on aptitude and basics. Product companies and startups hire fewer freshers and usually test coding and problem solving more deeply. Neither is "better" — many careers start in services and move later.

Worked example

Adapt it to your situation
Anu (B.Tech, 2026 batch) — her first 3 months of applications:

Campus:   2 drives via placement cell  → cleared 1 online test, rejected in technical round Off-campus:
  • 25 applications on company careers pages
  • 8 applications through Infopark / Technopark job boards
  • 3 referrals from seniors on LinkedIn
  • 1 walk-in drive in Kakkanad
Result:   4 online tests → 3 interviews → 1 offer (via a referral)

What to notice

• Offers usually come from volume plus focus: dozens of applications, a handful of interviews.
• Referrals and park job boards had a much better hit rate than cold applications — use every route, not just one.
• A rejection in one round is normal; the next company may test different things.

Names, companies and numbers are fictional examples. Always confirm details on the employer's official site.

Key points

  • Use both campus and off-campus routes.
  • Typical rounds: screen → online test → technical → HR.
  • Service companies test breadth; product companies test depth.
  • Check each company's own careers page for its current process.

Quick check

Which round usually comes first after your application is accepted?

Most fresher processes filter large numbers with an online test before any interview.

Practice task

List ten companies you'd like to join. For each, note whether it is services, product or startup, where it is located, and the official careers page link.

Interview corner

Why do you want to join a service company / a startup?

Be honest and specific: services offer structured training and exposure to many clients; startups offer ownership and speed. Link your answer to what you want to learn in the first two years.

Are you open to relocation and shifts?

Answer truthfully. If you have limits, say so politely with the reason — a mismatch found after joining is worse for both sides.
