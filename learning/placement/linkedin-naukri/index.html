LinkedIn and job-portal profiles recruiters find

Headline, skills, keywords and activity that make recruiters contact you.

← PreviousA one-page fresher resume that passes ATS Next lesson →Online assessments: aptitude, coding and proctoring

Recruiters search LinkedIn and job portals with keywords, location and experience filters. If your profile doesn't contain the words they search for, you won't appear — however good you are.

LinkedIn essentials

  • Photo — clear, plain background, professional clothing.
  • Headline — not just "Student". Use target role + key skills: "Aspiring Java Developer | Spring Boot, SQL, Git | B.Tech 2026".
  • About — four or five lines: what you do, what you've built, what you're looking for.
  • Skills — add the exact tools from job posts you target; ask classmates to endorse the top three.
  • Projects and certificates — with GitHub or live links.
  • Open to work — set role titles and locations (Kochi, Trivandrum, Bengaluru, remote).

Job portals

On portals, keep your profile complete and updated: many portals rank recently updated profiles higher in recruiter searches. Fill the "key skills" field with real tools, set preferred locations, and upload the same resume you use elsewhere.

Being active helps: share a project you built or a short note on something you learned, and comment thoughtfully on posts from people in roles you want.

Worked example

Adapt it to your situation
Before:  "Student at ABC College of Engineering"

After:   "Aspiring Frontend Developer | React, JavaScript, Tailwind | B.Tech CSE 2026 | Open to Kochi & Bengaluru"

About (after):
"Final-year CSE student who enjoys building fast, accessible web apps. I've built a job-tracker app (React + Node) used by 40 classmates and contributed bug fixes to an open-source UI library. Looking for a frontend or full-stack fresher role from July 2026."

What to notice

• The new headline contains the words recruiters type: role, React, JavaScript, location.
• The About section gives proof (a project with users, open source) instead of adjectives like "hard-working".
• It states availability, which recruiters filter on.

Names, companies and numbers are fictional examples. Always confirm details on the employer's official site.

Key points

  • Headline = target role + key skills.
  • Use the exact tool names recruiters search for.
  • Keep job-portal profiles updated regularly.
  • Show proof: projects, links, numbers.

Quick check

Why does a keyword-rich headline matter?

Recruiter searches match keywords; your headline and skills are heavily weighted.

Practice task

Rewrite your LinkedIn headline and About section using the pattern above. Search LinkedIn for your target role in your city and compare your profile with three people already in that job.

Interview corner

How do you keep your skills up to date?

Mention a concrete habit — a weekly project, a course you finished, newsletters or communities you follow — and one thing you learned recently.

Can you share your GitHub or portfolio?

Have a clean link ready. Pin your two best repositories with a README that explains what the project does and how to run it.
