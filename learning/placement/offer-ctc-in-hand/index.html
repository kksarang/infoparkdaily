Offer letters, CTC and in-hand salary

Read an offer letter, understand CTC vs take-home pay, and check bonds and notice periods.

← PreviousA 30-day off-campus job search plan Next lesson →Spotting fake jobs and paid-placement scams

An offer's headline number is usually the CTC (cost to company) — everything the company spends on you in a year. Your monthly in-hand (take-home) pay is lower because CTC includes items you don't receive as monthly cash.

What CTC often includes

  • Basic salary and allowances (HRA, special allowance) — paid monthly.
  • Employer's PF contribution — goes to your provident fund account, not your bank.
  • Gratuity — paid only after you complete the eligible years of service.
  • Variable pay / bonus — depends on performance; may be paid yearly or quarterly.
  • Insurance and one-time joining bonuses.

From the monthly gross, deductions such as your own PF contribution, professional tax (a small state tax) and any income tax (TDS) are taken. Ask HR for the salary break-up if it isn't in the letter.

Check before you accept

  • Role, location, joining date and probation period.
  • Service agreements / bonds — the amount, the period, and what happens if you leave early. Read them carefully.
  • Notice period after confirmation.
  • The offer comes from an official company email domain — never pay any fee to receive or confirm an offer.

More detail: offer letter and notice period guide.

Monthly (approx.) Gross cash  = (1,68,000 + 84,000 + 1,27,840) / 12  ≈ 31,650 − Employee PF (12% of basic) = 14,000 × 12%        ≈  1,680 − Professional tax (state rules)                   ≈    200 − Income tax (often nil at this level; check the current year's rules) In-hand                                            ≈ 29,800

What to notice

• In-hand (~₹29,800) is clearly less than CTC ÷ 12 (₹35,000).
• Employer PF is part of CTC but goes to your PF account, not salary.
• Exact deductions depend on the company's structure and the tax rules for the year — always ask HR for your own break-up.

Names, companies and numbers are fictional examples. Always confirm details on the employer's official site.

Key points

  • CTC is not your monthly take-home.
  • Ask for the salary break-up.
  • Read bond and notice-period clauses carefully.
  • Real offers never ask you to pay a fee.

Quick check

Why is in-hand salary lower than CTC ÷ 12?

CTC counts all employer costs, some of which are not monthly cash, and your gross pay has PF and tax deductions.

Practice task

Take any offer or a sample break-up and calculate the monthly in-hand: gross cash ÷ 12, minus employee PF, professional tax and estimated tax.

Interview corner

What are your salary expectations?

Research the market range for the role and city, give a realistic range, and add that you're open to discussion based on the full offer and learning opportunity.

Can you join in two weeks?

Answer based on your real situation: notice period, exams, relocation. If you need more time, explain why and propose a date.
