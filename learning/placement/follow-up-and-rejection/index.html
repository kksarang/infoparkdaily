Follow-ups, rejections and trying again

When and how to follow up, how to ask for feedback, and how to improve after a no.

← PreviousSpotting fake jobs and paid-placement scams You finished Placement Prep →Explore all learning

Most applications end in silence or a rejection — that's normal for everyone. What matters is following up professionally and improving each time.

Following up

  • After an interview, send a short thank-you email the same day, mentioning one thing you discussed.
  • If the recruiter gave a timeline, wait until it passes, then send one polite follow-up.
  • Follow up at most twice. After that, move on and keep applying.

After a rejection

  1. Reply politely and thank them — recruiters remember courteous candidates for future roles.
  2. Ask for one or two points of feedback; some will share.
  3. Write your own notes immediately: which questions went well, which didn't.
  4. Turn the weak areas into a one-week practice plan.
  5. Check the company's re-application rules — many allow you to apply again after a waiting period.

Worked example

Adapt it to your situation
Thank-you email (same day):

Subject: Thank you — Junior QA Engineer interview

"Dear Ms. Priya,
Thank you for speaking with me today about the Junior QA Engineer role. I enjoyed discussing how your team tests the mobile banking app, especially the regression suite you're automating. I'm excited about the chance to contribute to it. Best regards, Anu Kumar | +91 98xxx xxxxx"

Follow-up (after the promised date has passed):
"Dear Ms. Priya, I wanted to check whether there's any update on the Junior QA Engineer role. I'm still very interested. Thank you!"

What to notice

• Both emails are short, polite and specific to the conversation.
• The thank-you mentions a real detail, showing genuine interest.
• The follow-up waits for the promised timeline and doesn't pressure.

Names, companies and numbers are fictional examples. Always confirm details on the employer's official site.

Key points

  • Send a thank-you email the same day.
  • Follow up once after the promised timeline, at most twice.
  • Ask for feedback politely after a rejection.
  • Turn every rejection into a short practice plan.

Quick check

When is the right time for a follow-up email after an interview?

Waiting for the stated timeline is respectful; one polite follow-up after it is professional.

Practice task

After your next interview, write down every question asked within an hour. Mark each as strong, okay or weak, and plan practice for the weak ones.

Interview corner

Tell me about a time you failed.

Use STAR: a real setback, what you did, and — most importantly — what you learned and changed afterwards.

You applied here before. What has changed since?

Point to concrete improvements: new skills, projects, certifications or experience gained since your last application.
