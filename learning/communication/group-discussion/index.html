Group discussions

How GDs are evaluated, starting well, adding value, handling dominant speakers and summarising.

← PreviousBehavioural answers with STAR Next lesson →Professional emails & messages

Many campus drives and BPO, banking and management roles use a group discussion (GD): 6–12 candidates discuss a topic for 10–15 minutes while evaluators watch.

What evaluators look for

  • Relevant content with facts or examples — not just opinions.
  • Clear communication — short points, good pace, simple English.
  • Listening and building on others' points (“Adding to what Ravi said…”).
  • Leadership — bringing the group back on track, inviting quiet members, summarising.

Do and don't

  • Do speak early, two or three times, with quality points.
  • Don't shout, interrupt mid-sentence or repeat what others said.
  • If you don't know the topic, listen first and then summarise or connect points.

Model answer

Adapt it — don't memorise word for word
Topic: "Should companies allow permanent work from home?"

Opening (if you start):
"I'd like to begin by looking at this from three sides — employees, companies and customers. For employees, work from home saves travel time; surveys during 2020–21 showed many people valued that highly. But for companies, collaboration and training new freshers can be harder remotely."

Building on others:
"I agree with Meera about productivity, and I'd add that it depends on the role — support and coding can work well remotely, while hardware or lab work can't."

Summarising (near the end):
"To sum up, the group agrees that a hybrid model balances flexibility and collaboration, with the exact mix decided by the type of role."

Why it works

• The opening gives a structure the group can follow.
• Building on a named person shows listening and teamwork.
• The summary is neutral and covers the group's view, not only your own — evaluators notice this.

Names, companies and figures are fictional. Replace them with your real details and practise saying it aloud.

Key points

  • Evaluators score content, communication, listening and leadership.
  • Speak early with structured, factual points.
  • Build on others' points by name.
  • Offer a balanced summary at the end.

Quick check

You don't know much about the GD topic. What's the best approach?

Listening and then connecting or summarising others' points still shows communication and leadership.

Practice task

Pick a topic such as 'AI will create more jobs than it removes', write three points for each side, and practise a 30-second opening and a 30-second summary.

Interview corner

How do you handle someone who dominates a group discussion?

Wait for a pause, acknowledge their point briefly, then bring in your view or invite others: "That's a good point. I'd like to hear what others think about cost." Stay polite and never argue personally.

What's the difference between a GD and a debate?

A debate is about winning your side. A GD is a collaborative discussion where the group explores the topic and ideally reaches a balanced view; evaluators reward teamwork as much as content.
