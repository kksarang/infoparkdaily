Tell me about yourself

A 60–90 second introduction using present → past → future, for freshers and experienced candidates.

← Back toCommunication home Next lesson →Answering common HR questions

“Tell me about yourself” opens almost every interview. It's not a request for your life story — it's a short professional pitch that shows why you fit this role.

The structure: present → past → future

  1. Present — who you are now: your degree or current role, and your main skills.
  2. Past — one or two achievements that prove those skills (a project, internship or result with a number).
  3. Future — why you want this role at this company.

Keep it to 60–90 seconds. Don't repeat your whole resume, don't start with family details or hobbies, and end on the role — that invites the next question.

Model answer

Adapt it — don't memorise word for word
Fresher (B.Tech, applying for a junior developer role):

"Good morning. I'm Anu Kumar, a final-year B.Tech Computer Science student from Kochi. I enjoy building web applications, and I've worked mainly with JavaScript, React and Node.js.

For my final-year project, my team of three built a job-tracking web app. I handled the React frontend and the login API, and we got 40 classmates to use it during placement season.

I also completed a two-month internship where I fixed UI bugs and wrote unit tests for a client dashboard.

I'm applying for this role because your team builds products with React and Node, and I want to grow as a full-stack developer in a product company like yours."

Why it works

• Opens with name, education and core skills — relevant to the job.
• Gives proof: a project with a clear personal role and a number (40 users).
• Ends by linking the candidate's skills to the company — a natural lead-in to the next question.
• About 70 seconds when spoken at a calm pace.

Names, companies and figures are fictional. Replace them with your real details and practise saying it aloud.

Key points

  • Use present → past → future.
  • Keep it 60–90 seconds.
  • Include one or two achievements with numbers.
  • End with why you want this role.

Quick check

What should 'Tell me about yourself' focus on?

Interviewers want a concise pitch showing your skills, proof and motivation for the role.

Practice task

Write your own introduction using the three-part structure, record it on your phone, and cut anything that doesn't help you get this specific job.

Interview corner

Walk me through your resume.

Use the same structure in time order: education or first role, the most relevant experiences with one result each, and what you're looking for now. Keep it to about two minutes and point to the parts that match the job.

Why should we hire you?

Connect three things: the role's key needs (from the job description), your matching skills with proof, and your attitude — for example quick learning or ownership. Keep it specific to this job, not generic.
