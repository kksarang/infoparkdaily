Averages & mixtures

Average formula, effect of adding/removing items, and weighted averages.

← Ratio, proportion & partnership Time & work →
  • Average = sum of values / number of values, so Sum = Average × Count.
  • Adding a value above the average raises it; the change in average = (new value − old average) / new count.
  • Weighted average of groups: (n₁a₁ + n₂a₂) / (n₁ + n₂).
  • Average of consecutive numbers = average of the first and last.
  • Alligation (mixing two prices to get a mean): ratio = (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper) for cheaper : dearer.

Example: The average age of 10 employees is 28. When the manager joins, the average becomes 29. Find the manager's age.

  1. Old sum = 10 × 28 = 280.
  2. New sum = 11 × 29 = 319.
  3. Manager = 319 − 280.

Answer: 39 years

Example: In what ratio must rice at ₹40/kg be mixed with rice at ₹60/kg to get a mixture worth ₹45/kg?

  1. Cheaper : dearer = (60 − 45) : (45 − 40) = 15 : 5.

Answer: 3 : 1

Key points

  • Sum = Average × Count — work with sums.
  • Weighted average for groups of different sizes.
  • Alligation gives mixing ratios quickly.

Quick check

Average of 5 numbers is 20. If one number 30 is removed, the new average is…

Sum 100 − 30 = 70; 70 / 4 = 17.5.

Practice task

A batsman's average over 20 innings is 42. How many runs must he score in the 21st innings to raise his average to 44?

Show answer

Needed sum = 21 × 44 = 924; current = 20 × 42 = 840; runs = 84.

Interview corner

Class A (30 students) averages 60, class B (20) averages 70. Combined average?

(30×60 + 20×70) / 50 = 3200/50 = 64.

What is the average of the first 50 natural numbers?

(1 + 50) / 2 = 25.5.
