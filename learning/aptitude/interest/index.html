Simple & compound interest

SI and CI formulas, compounding periods and the CI − SI shortcut.

← Time, speed & distance Number series & number system →
  • Simple interest: SI = P × R × T / 100; amount = P + SI.
  • Compound interest: A = P (1 + R/100)ᵀ; CI = A − P.
  • Half-yearly compounding: use R/2 and 2T; quarterly: R/4 and 4T.
  • For 2 years, CI − SI = P (R/100)².
  • Money doubles under SI in 100 / R years; under CI roughly 72 / R years (rule of 72).

Example: Find the simple interest on ₹25,000 at 8% per year for 3 years.

  1. SI = 25,000 × 8 × 3 / 100.

Answer: ₹6,000

Example: Find the compound interest on ₹10,000 at 10% per year for 2 years.

  1. A = 10,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 12,100.
  2. CI = 12,100 − 10,000.

Answer: ₹2,100 (SI would be ₹2,000; difference = 10,000 × 0.01 = ₹100)

Key points

  • SI grows linearly; CI grows on the growing amount.
  • Adjust rate and periods for half-yearly/quarterly.
  • 2-year CI − SI = P(R/100)².

Quick check

At what SI rate does money double in 8 years?

Interest = principal in 8 years → R = 100/8 = 12.5%.

Practice task

The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 2 years at 5% is ₹50. Find the sum.

Show answer

P × (5/100)² = 50 → P × 0.0025 = 50 → P = ₹20,000.

Interview corner

Why do banks quote an effective annual rate?

With more frequent compounding the actual yearly growth exceeds the nominal rate; the effective rate makes products comparable.

Roughly how long to double money at 6% compound?

Rule of 72: 72 / 6 ≈ 12 years.
