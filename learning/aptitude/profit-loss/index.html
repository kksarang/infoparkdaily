Profit, loss & discount

Cost price, selling price, marked price and discount calculations.

← Percentages Ratio, proportion & partnership →
  • CP = cost price, SP = selling price, MP = marked (list) price.
  • Profit = SP − CP; Profit % = Profit / CP × 100 (always on CP).
  • SP = CP × (1 + profit%/100); SP = CP × (1 − loss%/100).
  • Discount is on MP: SP = MP × (1 − discount%/100).
  • Successive discounts a% and b% equal a single discount of a + b − ab/100.
  • Selling two items at the same SP, one at x% profit and one at x% loss, always gives a loss of x²/100%.

Example: A laptop bought for ₹40,000 is sold for ₹46,000. Find the profit percent.

  1. Profit = 46,000 − 40,000 = ₹6,000.
  2. Profit % = 6,000 / 40,000 × 100.

Answer: 15%

Example: A shop marks a phone at ₹20,000 and gives successive discounts of 10% and 5%. What does the customer pay?

  1. Single equivalent discount = 10 + 5 − 50/100 = 14.5%.
  2. SP = 20,000 × (1 − 0.145) = 20,000 × 0.855.

Answer: ₹17,100

Key points

  • Profit/loss % is on cost price.
  • Discount % is on marked price.
  • Same SP, x% gain and x% loss → x²/100 % loss overall.

Quick check

Two discounts of 20% and 10% equal a single discount of…

20 + 10 − (20 × 10)/100 = 28%.

Practice task

A trader marks goods 40% above cost and gives a 25% discount. Find his profit or loss percent.

Show answer

Take CP = 100 → MP = 140 → SP = 140 × 0.75 = 105. Profit = 5%.

Interview corner

If CP of 12 items equals SP of 10 items, what is the profit %?

Let each CP = 1: CP of 10 items = 10, SP of 10 items = 12. Profit = 2/10 × 100 = 20%.

Is a 10% markup followed by a 10% discount break-even?

No: 100 → 110 → 99, a 1% loss.
