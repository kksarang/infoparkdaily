Percentages

Percent basics, fraction shortcuts, increase/decrease and successive changes.

← Aptitude home Profit, loss & discount →

“Per cent” means “out of 100”. Most company aptitude rounds (TCS, Infosys, UST, campus drives) open with percentage questions because they underpin profit, interest and data interpretation.

Key facts

  • x% of y = x × y / 100 — and x% of y = y% of x (8% of 50 = 50% of 8 = 4).
  • Fraction shortcuts: 50% = ½, 25% = ¼, 20% = ⅕, 12.5% = ⅛, 33⅓% = ⅓, 16⅔% = ⅙.
  • Increase by r%: multiply by (1 + r/100). Decrease by r%: multiply by (1 − r/100).
  • Successive changes a% and b%: net change = a + b + ab/100 (use negative values for decreases).
  • If A is r% more than B, then B is r/(100 + r) × 100% less than A.

Example: A salary of ₹40,000 is increased by 10% and then reduced by 10%. What is the final salary?

  1. Net change = 10 + (−10) + (10 × −10)/100 = −1%.
  2. Final = 40,000 × 0.99.

Answer: ₹39,600 — a 1% fall, not back to ₹40,000.

Example: Anu's marks are 25% more than Rahul's. By what percent are Rahul's marks less than Anu's?

  1. Use r/(100 + r) × 100 with r = 25.
  2. 25/125 × 100 = 20.

Answer: 20% less.

Key points

  • Convert common percentages to fractions for speed.
  • Successive change: a + b + ab/100.
  • ‘More than’ and ‘less than’ use different bases.

Quick check

A price rises 20% and then falls 20%. The net change is…

20 − 20 + (20 × −20)/100 = −4%.

Practice task

A town's population of 50,000 grows 10% in year one and 20% in year two. What is it after two years?

Show answer

50,000 × 1.1 × 1.2 = 66,000 (net growth 10 + 20 + 2 = 32%).

Interview corner

How do you find what percent 45 is of 180?

45/180 × 100 = 25%. Spot it faster: 45 is a quarter of 180.

Why isn't a 50% loss recovered by a 50% gain?

The gain is calculated on the smaller amount: 100 → 50 → 75. You need a 100% gain to get back to 100.
