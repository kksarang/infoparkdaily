How to email a recruiter for Kerala IT jobs

Updated 20 October 2026 · 12 min read · By InfoparkDaily editorial

When a park listing or company notice says “mail your CV”, a short clear email beats a long story. This guide gives you a fill-in checklist and eight copy-paste templates for Infopark, Technopark, Cyberpark, and other Kerala IT applications. Replace every [bracket] with your details. Only send to the address printed on the official posting.

Build your resume Browse open jobs

1. Fill this checklist before you send

Write these down once. Use the same facts in the subject, body, and PDF filename.

  • Recruiter or HR name — if known; otherwise use “Hiring Team”.
  • Exact role title — copy from the posting, do not invent a nearby title.
  • Company name — as printed on the listing.
  • Where you saw it — Infopark board, Technopark board, company careers page, or InfoparkDaily (then confirm on the official link).
  • City or park — e.g. Kochi · Infopark Kakkanad, Trivandrum · Technopark.
  • Experience band — Fresher, 0–1 year, or N years.
  • One or two proof lines — a skill, project, or result that matches the post.
  • Phone and professional email — active number; avoid joke or temporary addresses.
  • One PDF resume — name it [YourName]_[Role]_[Company].pdf unless the notice asks for more documents.
  • Job ID or reference — include it if the posting prints one.

2. Subject line formula

Recruiters filter by subject. Keep it boring and exact:

Application — [Role title] — [Your Name]

If a job ID is printed:

Application — [Role title] — [Job ID] — [Your Name]

Example:

Application — Junior Software Engineer — Ananya Menon

3. Five rules that keep you safe and readable

  1. Attach one PDF unless the notice asks for certificates, portfolio, or a form.
  2. Use the exact role title from the posting in the subject and the first sentence.
  3. Send a separate email per role if you apply to more than one opening in the same company.
  4. Save the sent mail and any auto-reply — that is your proof of apply date.
  5. Mail only the address on the official posting. Check the domain looks like the company (not a free random inbox). Never pay for a job or interview, and never send OTP, Aadhaar, or bank details by email. Read Verify jobs before you apply.

4. Email templates

Open a template, replace every [bracket], then paste into your mail client. Keep the body short — park HR often decides in under a minute whether to open the attachment. Build or refresh the PDF in the resume builder first.

1. Fresher — apply to a posted role

When to use: You are a fresher or recent graduate applying to a role that asks for a CV by email.

Subject

Application — [Role title] — [Your Name]

Body

Dear [Hiring Team / Recruiter name],

I am applying for the [Role title] role at [Company], as listed on [Infopark board / Technopark board / company careers page]. I am a fresher based in [City / park], with a [degree, year].

Relevant preparation: [skill or project 1]; [skill or project 2]. My resume (PDF) is attached.

Phone: [phone]
Email: [email]

Thank you for your time. I am happy to share any certificates named in the notice.

Regards,
[Your Name]

Common mistakes: Writing a full autobiography; applying to “any open role” in the same mail; attaching Word plus PDF plus screenshots.

2. Experienced — apply to a posted role

When to use: You have clear years of experience and the posting asks for email apply.

Subject

Application — [Role title] — [Your Name]

Body

Dear [Hiring Team / Recruiter name],

Please find my application for [Role title] at [Company], listed on [source]. I have [N] years of experience in [domain / stack], currently based in [City].

Recent work: [result or responsibility 1]; [result or responsibility 2]. Resume PDF attached. Notice period: [immediate / N days].

Phone: [phone]
Email: [email]

Happy to join a call at your convenience.

Regards,
[Your Name]

Common mistakes: Inflating years; pasting the full LinkedIn “About” section; omitting notice period when the post says “immediate joiners”.

3. Light experience / 0–1 year

When to use: The post allows freshers or 0–1 year, or you have a short internship or trainee stint.

Subject

Application — [Role title] — [Your Name]

Body

Dear [Hiring Team / Recruiter name],

I am applying for [Role title] at [Company] (seen on [source]). I have [0–1 year / internship months] of experience and am based in [City].

Fit for this post: [matching skill or tool from the JD]; [project, internship, or coursework]. Resume PDF attached.

Phone: [phone]
Email: [email]

Thank you for considering my application.

Regards,
[Your Name]

Common mistakes: Claiming “2+ years” for a short internship; ignoring required tools listed in the post.

4. Walk-in confirmation

When to use: The notice asks you to email before attending, or to confirm date, time, and venue.

Subject

Walk-in confirmation — [Role title] — [Date] — [Your Name]

Body

Dear [Hiring Team / Recruiter name],

I would like to attend the walk-in for [Role title] at [Company] on [date] at [venue / building if printed], as listed on [source].

I am a [fresher / N years] candidate based in [City]. Resume PDF attached as requested. Please confirm if I should report at a specific time window.

Phone: [phone]
Email: [email]

Thank you.

Regards,
[Your Name]

Common mistakes: Emailing a WhatsApp-only “HR” number that is not on the official notice; arriving without checking the building the morning of the drive. See walk-in tips.

5. Referral / named contact

When to use: Someone shared a specific HR or recruiter email and you are applying to a named role (still verify the address against the company domain when you can).

Subject

Application — [Role title] — referred by [Referrer Name] — [Your Name]

Body

Dear [Recruiter name],

[Referrer Name] suggested I write regarding the [Role title] opening at [Company]. I am based in [City] with [fresher / N years] experience in [domain].

Brief fit: [proof line 1]; [proof line 2]. Resume PDF attached. Happy to share more details if useful.

Phone: [phone]
Email: [email]

Thank you for your time.

Regards,
[Your Name]

Common mistakes: Name-dropping without a real referral; using a personal Gmail that claims to be “company HR” without checking. Prefer the careers or park listing address when both exist.

6. Follow-up after 7–10 days

When to use: You already applied, the posting is still open, and you have had no reply. One polite follow-up is enough.

Subject

Follow-up — Application — [Role title] — [Your Name]

Body

Dear [Hiring Team / Recruiter name],

I applied for [Role title] at [Company] on [apply date] (email / portal confirmation saved). The role still appears open on [source], so I am writing once to check if you need any further information.

Resume was attached earlier; I can resend if helpful. Thank you for your consideration.

Phone: [phone]
Email: [email]

Regards,
[Your Name]

Common mistakes: Daily follow-ups; CC’ing five people in the same company; sounding frustrated in the first line.

7. Thank-you after interview

When to use: Same day or next working day after a phone, video, or campus interview.

Subject

Thank you — [Role title] interview — [Your Name]

Body

Dear [Interviewer / Recruiter name],

Thank you for speaking with me today about the [Role title] role at [Company]. I appreciated the discussion on [topic you discussed].

I remain interested in the opportunity. Please let me know if you need any additional documents.

Regards,
[Your Name]
[phone] · [email]

Common mistakes: Long renegotiation of salary in the thank-you mail; mass-sending the same note with the wrong company name.

8. Decline or withdraw

When to use: You accepted another offer, cannot join on the required date, or no longer want to continue. Keep it short and polite.

Subject

Withdrawing application — [Role title] — [Your Name]

Body

Dear [Hiring Team / Recruiter name],

Thank you for considering my application for [Role title] at [Company]. I need to withdraw my application at this time [optional: due to another commitment / notice period].

I appreciate your time and wish the team well with the hire.

Regards,
[Your Name]

Common mistakes: Ghosting after a scheduled interview; criticising the process in the withdraw mail.

5. What not to write

  • Subject lines like “Urgent”, “ASAP”, or “Job please” with no role title.
  • Fake years of experience or tools you cannot discuss in a live round.
  • Mass BCC to every careers@ address you found online.
  • Passwords, OTPs, Aadhaar copies, or bank details in the first mail.
  • Payment for “registration”, “slot booking”, or “background check” to a personal account.
